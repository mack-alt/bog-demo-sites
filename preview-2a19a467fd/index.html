The math

How many new clients does it take?

An average recurring client is worth about $350 a month in the price book. So, with no other changes:

$10,000 gap ÷ about $350 = about 29 new clients

That is a lot of new clients. The good news is that you don't have to find all of them. Raising the clients who are underpriced closes part of the gap without adding a single new home.

Ideas to explore

Have you ever considered…

Beyond the six plays, here are four kinds of customers who often value a reliable cleaner. They're ideas to explore together, not commitments. You'd pick which ones, if any, feel right for you, and we'd start small.

1

Light post-remodel and kitchen/bath turnovers

The opportunityContractors, flooring installers, and remodelers tend to dread the final detail clean, because it holds up their walkthrough and their payment. Your crew could take on light drywall dust, window stickers, and floor polishing, and this kind of work may be worth a premium business rate to them.

How I'd helpI'd reach out to local contractors by email and LinkedIn. I'd also set up the Front Desk so that when a contractor calls from a loud job site, someone answers, shares your post-remodel rate, and gets them on the schedule while you're out on jobs.

2

Premium real-estate move-in/move-outs

The opportunityRealtors and property managers need a home spotless before listing photos or handing over keys. They often pay well for a cleaner who makes them look good and hits their deadlines.

How I'd helpI'd build a simple “Realtor VIP booking” page and work on showing up when local agents search for turnover help. Calls would be answered and jobs booked quickly.

3

Small medical and aesthetics clinics

The opportunityMed-spas, private dental offices, and boutique clinics need daily or weekly cleaning. The square footage is often close to a house, and commercial budgets are often higher than residential ones.

How I'd helpI'd run local ads and reach out directly to clinic owners. The Front Desk would answer rate questions, so your brand looks as established as it is.

4

Short-term rentals (Airbnb/VRBO)

The opportunityHosts can lose ratings and bookings if a place isn't spotless by check-in, so they pay for reliability. You already clean Airbnbs, so this builds on what's working.

How I'd helpI'd set up local search campaigns to reach hosts who are looking for a cleaner, plus an easy way for a host to request a turnover that logs the job and alerts your crew.

Each of these is a direction to test, not a promise of any result. Pricing and scope would be written down first, the same way as everything else here.
